# Protocol guide

A plain-language guide to every protocol in this repository (54 total), generated from the JSON files in `protocols/`. **Do not edit by hand** — change the protocol JSON and run `npm run build`.

Protocols come in three kinds:

- **Daily** and **weekly** protocols are on your list on the scheduled days.
- **Conditional** protocols only appear when your ring data says they are relevant (for example, a poor night of recovery or a stressful day). The "When it appears" line says exactly what has to be true.

Signals marked *(phone-estimated)* are computed on your phone from your ring's heartbeats and movement. They are estimates, not medical measurements, and protocols that depend on them are marked **Experimental**.

## Sleep

### Morning Sunlight Exposure

*daily · foundation*

Expose your eyes to natural sunlight within the first 30 minutes of waking up to align your circadian rhythm, enhance mood, and improve alertness.

**What to do:**

- **Get at least 1000 lux of natural light exposure (ideally sunlight)** — 5–10 minutes on sunny days; 10–20 minutes on cloudy days, Within the first 30–60 minutes of waking

**Why:** Improves sleep quality by anchoring circadian rhythm; Boosts mood by increasing dopamine and serotonin levels; Enhances alertness and wakefulness in the morning.

**Learn more:** [How to Feel Energized & Sleep Better With One Morning Activity — Huberman Lab Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WDv4AWk0J3U) · [Using Light (Sunlight, Blue Light & Red Light) to Optimize Health — Huberman Lab Podcast](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/using-light-sunlight-blue-light-and-red-light-to-optimize-health) · [Andrew Huberman Swears by Morning Sunlight — Business Insider Profile](https://www.businessinsider.com/andrew-huberman-swears-by-morning-sunlight-healthy-circadian-clock-2023-11)

### NSDR for Severe Recovery Deficit

*conditional · foundation · 30-60 minutes · beginner*

Sleep duration, HRV, and resting heart rate have all moved sharply against your personal baseline — a severe, high-confidence recovery deficit. Recommends an extended NSDR session and postponing intense training for the day.

**When it appears** (Evidence-informed):

- sleep duration < 80% of rolling baseline **and** HRV < 80% of rolling baseline **and** resting HR > 110% of rolling baseline

**What to do:**

- **Extended NSDR / Yoga Nidra session** — 30-60 minutes, Morning, with an optional second short session in the evening
- **Postpone intense training** — For the day, All day

**Why:** Avoids training through a severe, multi-signal-confirmed autonomic strain state; Reduces risk of illness, injury, or prolonged recovery debt from pushing through this state; Extended NSDR provides a larger relative recovery benefit at this severity than the high-tier dose.

**Be careful if:**

- If this state recurs on consecutive days, this is a stronger signal to consult a physician or coach rather than repeatedly self-managing with NSDR alone
- Not a diagnosis of illness — if accompanied by other symptoms (fever, sore throat, unusual fatigue), treat as a possible early illness signal and prioritize rest/medical guidance over training decisions

**Learn more:** [Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) — Huberman Lab official page](https://www.hubermanlab.com/nsdr) · [NSDR Protocol by Huberman Lab](https://ai.hubermanlab.com/s/mUZwWStP)

### Evening Light Minimization

*daily · foundation · Ongoing evening practice · beginner*

Minimize bright artificial light exposure between 10 PM and 4 AM to protect melatonin secretion, support circadian health, and improve sleep quality.

**What to do:**

- **Dim overhead lights and use low-positioned lamps after sunset** — Evening hours, From sunset until bedtime
- **Use blue-light blocking or night mode on screens** — Evening hours, In the evening after sunset, especially 10 PM - 4 AM

**Why:** Protects melatonin production for healthy sleep onset; Reduces cortisol dysregulation from nighttime light; Improves sleep latency (time to fall asleep).

**Be careful if:**

- None — universally safe and beneficial
- Those with night-shift jobs should consult specialized circadian protocols

**Learn more:** [Using Light (Sunlight, Blue Light & Red Light) to Optimize Health — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/using-light-sunlight-blue-light-and-red-light-to-optimize-health) · [Toolkit for Sleep — Huberman Lab Newsletter](https://www.hubermanlab.com/newsletter/toolkit-for-sleep)

### Pre-Bed Wind-Down Activities Protocol

*daily · foundation · 30-60 minutes before bedtime · beginner*

Science-backed wind-down routine to prepare your body and mind for sleep by establishing consistent pre-bedtime activities that signal the transition to rest.

**What to do:**

- **Meditation practice** — 10-15 minutes, 30-60 minutes before bed
- **Light stretching routine** — 10-15 minutes, After meditation or as standalone activity
- **Reading (non-screen)** — 15-30 minutes, As part of wind-down routine
- **Listening to relaxing audio** — 10-20 minutes, During stretching or reading
- **Warm bath or shower** — 10-20 minutes, Early in wind-down routine
- **Mental visualization practice** — 5-10 minutes, Just before getting into bed

**Why:** Signals to your body that it's time to prepare for sleep; Activates parasympathetic nervous system for relaxation; Reduces mental chatter and racing thoughts.

**Be careful if:**

- Avoid vigorous exercise or stimulating activities
- Don't engage in work-related or stressful conversations
- Avoid bright screens and blue light exposure
- Skip caffeine or other stimulants during wind-down time
- Don't choose reading material that's too exciting or anxiety-provoking

### NSDR for High Recovery Deficit

*conditional · high-impact · 20-30 minutes · beginner*

Sleep duration, HRV, and resting heart rate have all moved meaningfully against your personal baseline in the same night — a three-signal, high-confidence recovery deficit. Recommends a longer NSDR session and reducing high-intensity training for the day.

**When it appears** (Evidence-informed):

- sleep duration < 85% of rolling baseline **and** HRV < 85% of rolling baseline **and** resting HR > 105% of rolling baseline

**What to do:**

- **Extended NSDR / Yoga Nidra session** — 20-30 minutes, Morning or early afternoon
- **Reduce high-intensity training** — For the day, All day

**Why:** Avoids compounding strain by adding hard training on top of a genuine multi-signal recovery deficit; Supports faster return to baseline HRV/RHR; Reduces injury and illness risk associated with training through significant autonomic strain.

**Be careful if:**

- Should not replace addressing the underlying cause of poor sleep if it recurs
- If this state persists for multiple consecutive days, treat as a stronger signal to scale back training volume, not just intensity, and consider consulting a coach or physician

*daily · high-impact · 5-10 minutes · beginner*

View sunlight in the late afternoon to signal your circadian clock that evening is approaching, which helps protect against the negative effects of artificial light at night on melatonin production.

### Address Poor Sleep Quality (Low Efficiency + Slow Onset)

*conditional · high-impact · Ongoing (evening + morning routine change) · beginner*

Sleep efficiency has dropped below your personal baseline at the same time sleep latency has risen above it — a combined signal of poor sleep quality (slow to fall asleep, then a larger share of time in bed spent awake) that can occur even when total time in bed looks adequate. Recommends the same behavioral first-line response as elevated sleep latency, with added emphasis on stress/arousal management.

**When it appears** (Evidence-informed):

- sleep efficiency < 90% of rolling baseline **and** sleep latency > 120% of rolling baseline

**What to do:**

- **Push caffeine intake earlier and add a firm cutoff** — Ongoing, Delay first caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking; no caffeine within 8-10 hours of target bedtime
- **Reduce evening bright/blue light and pre-sleep arousal** — 1-2 hours before bed, Evening
- **NSDR or brief relaxation practice before bed** — 10-15 minutes, Before bed

**Why:** Targets the shared arousal-related driver when both onset and efficiency are affected together, rather than treating them as separate issues; Behavioral-first, no supplements; Distinguishes this state from simple short-duration recovery deficit, which calls for a different response.

**Be careful if:**

- Not a diagnosis or treatment for insomnia or another sleep disorder
- If this combined pattern persists most nights over several weeks despite consistent behavioral changes, consider discussing with a physician or sleep specialist

**Learn more:** [Master Your Sleep & Be More Alert When Awake — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/master-your-sleep-and-be-more-alert-when-awake) · [Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) — Huberman Lab official page](https://www.hubermanlab.com/nsdr)

### Behavioral Reset for Elevated Sleep Latency

*conditional · high-impact · Ongoing (evening + morning routine change) · beginner*

Time-to-fall-asleep has moved meaningfully above your personal baseline for at least one night — a signal of elevated pre-sleep arousal (caffeine still active, late bright/blue light exposure, irregular bedtime) rather than a sleep disorder. Recommends behavioral adjustments first, not supplements.

**When it appears** (Evidence-informed):

- sleep latency > 130% of rolling baseline

**What to do:**

- **Push caffeine intake earlier and add a firm cutoff** — Ongoing, Delay first caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking; no caffeine within 8-10 hours of your target bedtime
- **Reduce evening bright/blue light exposure** — 1-2 hours before bed, Evening
- **Anchor a consistent bed and wake time** — Ongoing, Fixed times

**Why:** Addresses the most common behavioral drivers of elevated sleep latency before assuming anything is medically wrong; No supplements or medication involved — lowest-risk first response; Builds sleep-onset consistency over time rather than treating a single bad night in isolation.

**Be careful if:**

- Not a diagnosis or treatment for insomnia or another sleep disorder
- If elevated sleep latency persists most nights over several weeks despite consistent behavioral changes, consider discussing with a physician or sleep specialist rather than continuing to self-manage indefinitely

### Investigate Sleep Fragmentation Despite Adequate Duration

*conditional · supplemental · One-time reflection + ongoing evening adjustment · beginner*

Restless periods are elevated above your personal baseline while total sleep duration looks roughly normal — a pattern suggesting fragmented, lower-quality sleep rather than insufficient sleep. Recommends investigating likely causes rather than simply sleeping more, with relaxation practice as a secondary support.

**When it appears** (Product heuristic):

- restless periods > 130% of rolling baseline **and** sleep duration >= 95% of rolling baseline (total sleep roughly normal)

**What to do:**

- **Review likely fragmentation triggers from the previous evening** — 5 minutes, Anytime
- **Adjust the sleep environment and evening stimulation** — Ongoing, Evening / bedroom setup
- **NSDR or brief relaxation practice before bed (secondary)** — 10-15 minutes, Before bed

**Why:** Directs attention to specific, fixable causes instead of a generic 'get more sleep' response that doesn't match this pattern; Avoids over-recommending relaxation practices as a first-line fix when a concrete environmental or timing cause is more likely; Low-cost reflection step before considering anything more involved.

**Be careful if:**

- This is not a sleep disorder diagnosis. If restless/fragmented sleep of this kind recurs frequently (e.g. most nights over several weeks) despite addressing obvious environmental and timing factors, consider discussing with a physician, since conditions such as sleep apnea or periodic limb movements can present this way and require clinical evaluation
- Not a substitute for addressing a real total-sleep-duration deficit if one is also present — check this rule alongside duration/recovery signals, not in isolation

**Learn more:** [Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) — Huberman Lab official page](https://www.hubermanlab.com/nsdr) · [Master Your Sleep & Be More Alert When Awake — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/master-your-sleep-and-be-more-alert-when-awake)

*daily · foundation · N/A — timing adjustment · beginner*

Delay your first caffeine intake to 90-120 minutes after waking to allow natural cortisol-driven alertness to peak, then use caffeine to extend focus without an afternoon crash.

- **Delay caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking** — N/A, 90-120 minutes after waking up

**Be careful if:**

### Nasal Breathing Practice

*daily · high-impact · Ongoing daily practice · beginner*

Deliberately practice nasal breathing during the day and especially during low-to-moderate exercise to improve oxygen delivery, filter air, and activate the parasympathetic nervous system.

**What to do:**

- **Breathe through your nose during daily activities and exercise** — Ongoing, Throughout the day, especially during low-moderate intensity exercise

### Stress Reset: Physiological Sighs

*conditional · high-impact · 1-5 minutes, up to 3 times · beginner*

Your ring has spent a large part of today with an elevated heart rate while you were sitting still, which is a pattern that often goes with stress. A few minutes of physiological sighs is a fast, low-effort way to bring arousal back down.

**When it appears** (Experimental (thresholds are first guesses)):

- 60+ minutes of stillness with a raised heart rate today (phone-estimated)
- **or** 45+ minutes of stillness with a raised heart rate today (phone-estimated) **and** last night's HRV < 90% of rolling baseline

**What to do:**

- **Physiological sigh reset** — 1-5 minutes, Whenever you notice tension; good before a meeting or after a stressful exchange
- **Step outside for a short walk** — 10 minutes, Any time, ideally away from screens

**Why:** Quickly lowers physiological arousal without needing equipment or time set aside; Breathing with an emphasis on the exhale is associated with a calmer heart rate and better mood; Gives a concrete response to a stress signal instead of just noticing it.

**Be careful if:**

- Stop and breathe normally if you feel lightheaded
- The stress estimate is approximate and can be raised by caffeine, illness, or dehydration as well as stress
- If you feel persistently anxious or unwell, talk to a doctor or mental health professional rather than relying on an app estimate

**Learn more:** [Tools for Managing Stress & Anxiety — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/tools-for-managing-stress-and-anxiety) · [How to Breathe Correctly for Optimal Health, Mood, Learning & Performance — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/how-to-breathe-correctly-for-optimal-health-mood-learning-and-performance)

## Nutrition

### Hydration & Electrolyte Protocol

*daily · foundation · Throughout the day · beginner*

Optimize daily hydration with water and electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) to support brain function, physical performance, and energy levels throughout the day.

**What to do:**

- **Drink water with electrolytes upon waking** — First 30 minutes of the day, Immediately upon waking
- **Maintain hydration throughout the day** — All day, Spread evenly across waking hours

**Why:** Improves cognitive function and focus; Supports physical performance and endurance; Prevents headaches and fatigue from dehydration.

**Be careful if:**

- Those with kidney disease should consult a physician about fluid and electrolyte intake
- Excessive water without electrolytes can cause hyponatremia
- Adjust intake based on activity level, climate, and body size

**Learn more:** [Optimize Your Water Quality & Intake for Health — Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/optimize-your-water-quality-and-intake-for-health)

### Omega-3 / EPA Supplementation

*daily · high-impact · Ongoing daily supplementation · beginner*

Daily supplementation with omega-3 fatty acids (targeting 1-2g EPA) to support mood, cardiovascular health, brain function, and reduce inflammation.

**What to do:**

- **Take omega-3 supplement with at least 1g EPA** — Immediate, With a meal containing fat for better absorption

**Why:** Significant antidepressant effects at 1g+ EPA/day; Reduces systemic inflammation; Supports cardiovascular health.

**Be careful if:**

- May interact with blood-thinning medications — consult physician
